Reality Made in the Encounter Milestone 2 · PRS Asia · Sept 2026

A living organism of work

Reality Made
in the Encounter

The intangible layer of this city, and my perception of it: the night, the sidewalk, the light, the hour when a place that repairs motorbikes fills with people eating. I believe that layer is what makes Ho Chi Minh City the place it is, and that it may be the part of it that does not survive being measured.

Yuri Frassis4017499 · DR207
Officine Gặpwith Afra Rebuscini, est. 2022

01 — Reality made in the encounter

I believe that reality is an artefact our perception creates.

The city I walk around in is not a thing sitting there waiting to be recorded accurately. It is something produced, continuously, in the encounter between a place and an instrument — a body, a habit, a camera, a category, a language. Change the instrument and you get a different city, and neither version is the false one.

That is not a dive into subjectivity. An impression cannot be examined. An instrument can: it can be built, published, handed to somebody else and argued with.

By instrument I mean anything standing between a place and a perception of it: a body, a habit, a lens, a language. A good one is not the accurate one but the one legible enough to be worked against.

Two conditions run under all of it, and neither was chosen. The night, which I did not choose as a method and for a long time did not think of as one. After moving to Saigon in 2022 walking after dark became a ritual, a way of being in the city more slowly. It does not subtract, it rearranges: surfaces dim, outlines blur, people occupy the street differently, and my own attention stops scanning and starts staying. Not belonging, so I have always had to pay attention. Things that might become second nature to someone from here, I have had to observe, understand, and slowly absorb. The city reaches me as sound and texture before it reaches me as meaning, and that is a way of approaching it rather than something missing.

The tools belong to the same family, and what makes them one is not that each frames perception differently. It is that each began as a way around something I did not have: the edges of a photograph, the short duration of a recording, a blurred image,1 the dark room of 2022.2 I photograph on a phone rather than a camera. A recording is short because my attention is, and because I am not trying to record the whole song somebody is singing at a karaoke machine — I want the thirty seconds that frame the atmosphere. None of these was chosen as a method. Each was a way of working around something missing, and each became a method at the point where I recognised what it was already doing. The instrument is not any one of these frames. It is the way they are brought together as a way of working, and the sound map is where that becomes something someone else can hold, read and work against.

1 My own way of photographing this city, because I am more interested in perception than in representation.   2 Crafting A Sonic City, 2022 — an installation where the audience sat with their eyes covered.

02 — Understanding through doing

Understanding through doing — how a practice comes to know a place it does not belong to.

Five techniques. The projects appear across all of them, doing different work in each.

Walking

Arriving on foot, at length, and usually late.

The same procedure in every city I have lived in: arrive somewhere I do not know, walk it at length, photograph what stops me, find the people who seem to be doing something interesting. Only then make anything. I did not recognise it as a procedure until recently.

Further back, in Rome in the 1990s, a group called Stalker made walking the city their primary method: collective, unhurried, attentive to the parts of a city that planning disregards. Francesco Careri and Lorenzo Romito describe their own role at Campo Boario as producing neither objects nor projects, only paths and relationships. That is the family I belong to, practices that treat being in a city, at length and on foot, as a legitimate way of producing knowledge. What does not transfer is the ground. They walked ground that had been let go of; I walk ground in full use, by people who are working.

Why walking, and not a survey? Because walking creates the possibility of encountering material I did not go looking for.

Recording

Recording the glimpse rather than surveying the whole.

The median duration of recordings on the sound map is thirty-six seconds. Seventy-eight per cent are under a minute. These are not field recordings in the ethnographic sense; nobody is documenting an hour of a market to capture its structure. The archive is made of attention spans.

What a glimpse can and cannot hold. It records what stopped me, which is a fact about attention rather than about the city. A glimpse does not tell you everything about the city. It tells you what caught your attention at a particular moment.

Collaborating

Nothing here was made alone.

Crafting A Sonic City was made with HUM Studios and mastered by Rabih Bardawill; the zine was designed by Jinny DH, and its Vietnamese edition translated by Khả-Nhân Trần and Khôi Minh; the dark rooms were built with the Blind Associations of Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Dạo Nghe happened inside MORUA's Trà đá Party. TUAR was run with the educators of Little Em's. The map goes to Nổ Cái Bùm with Vu's performance, and to Rare Sea Gallery from October.

The acknowledgement. Every project carries other people's names, and showing the work means making those collaborations visible.

Producing

Making a lot, together, and in public.

Between 2022 and 2025, Afra and I kept up a rhythm I can only see now: two sound exhibitions in two cities inside three weeks; an architecture week; a workshop with five-year-olds that ended in an exhibition; several sound walks and zines, then the Vietnamese editions; talks in Saigon, Bangkok and Trento; and the map. Alongside all the conversations and studio visits of the Gặp Series. The rhythm is the method — short cycles, published, then the next one.

Where this starts to fail. The book we attempted never saw the light, partly because we kept adding conditions for what it needed to become — more complete, more coherent, more carefully curated. The standard kept moving. What worked for the shorter cycles became harder to sustain when something asked us to stop, look back, and make sense of what we had already done. And everything gets recorded somehow — audio, photographs, notes — but much of it remains unpublished. The archive grows faster than we can return to it. The publishing runs behind the making, and the reason is not only perfectionism: two people do all of it. Every cycle creates the same choice: make the next thing, or stop long enough to give the last thing a form. We have mostly chosen to keep moving.

Earning trust

Slowly, in person, before anything is asked.

Nothing here began with a request. The architects opened their studios to the public because we had been visiting them for months first. Little Em's trusted us with fifteen children at a roundabout. The Blind Associations worked with us in both cities. The practitioners in the Gặp Series let a two-person practice with no institution behind it sit in their workshops and ask questions. I do not have a method for this. It runs on time and presence, and on the relationship going both ways: for years I was not asking anybody for anything. That is how a practice comes to know a place it does not belong to.

What this requires. Trust does not scale and cannot be transferred. Whatever else in this practice somebody could pick up and use, this part they would have to build themselves.

03 — The instrument

The instrument is the authored work. Not the recordings it holds, and not any one state of its classification.

Saigon_Miền Tây Sound Map, 2025– · 159 recordings · open contribution.

The instrument, live. Not documentation — the map itself. Open it in its own window ↗

It began in a dark room. Crafting A Sonic City, 2022: an audience with their eyes covered, listening to a version of the city. We had interviewed the Blind Associations of both cities twice by then, and we thought that taking one sense away and shifting attention entirely onto sound would let people enter the experience further. It worked, and then it was over — an efficient way of making an argument and a very inefficient way of keeping one. Everything technical about the map answers that: how do you make an atmosphere available to someone who was not there?

The recordings are evidence. The instrument is the work: the map itself, and the fact that anybody can open it and add to it. Publishing it is a claim, made in public, about what this city is made of.

How it actually works

What I decide is how the instrument is built. Each recording is a pin at the coordinates where it was made. The player gives title, category, date, duration, audio and, since July 2026, an optional photograph — and deliberately no waveform, which would make a thirty-six-second glimpse look like a specimen.

What happens after that is not mine. Anyone can open the map and record into it. Nothing is filtered and nothing waits for me: an upload goes straight onto the map. I occasionally record into it myself, like anybody else. The only power I keep afterwards is deletion, which I have not had to use.

And the instrument does not record who contributed. There is no name field on the upload form and no author field in the data, and that is deliberate. What matters here is what was recorded, not who uploaded it. The archive is meant to become large and collective, and a name attached to every file would make it a collection of authors rather than a collection of a city.

Inaudible in its own city. The archive originally served WebM — smaller, technically better, and inaudible on Safari on iOS. For a period a public archive of a Southeast Asian city could not be heard on the most common way of getting online in that city. Migrated to MP3, June 2026. The format decision was a distribution decision wearing technical clothes, and I did not see it until somebody could not hear it.

Its live present

The map enters Nổ Cái Bùm with a zine and a sound performance by Vu, a sound practitioner building his own modular synthesiser — and the person who told me I had stopped making recordings and started making an instrument. From October, monthly sound walks and workshops with Rare Sea Gallery.

The instrument is the work, and it is not mine alone

Most of my hours go into building the thing itself rather than recording: the software, what a recording is allowed to carry, who can contribute, how it gets activated.

I build it daily, in conversation with an AI, the way Vu solders pieces onto an open circuit: adding, connecting, testing, and leaving some connections unresolved.

The instrument does not work when it sits. It does its real work when Afra builds activation events around it — staged, played, walked with. Performing it is not my skill. Officine Gặp is a two-person operation in which building and activating are different jobs.

Where her doctorate ends and mine begins is a line we have had to draw ourselves. I build the part underneath — the platform, what a recording can carry, who can press upload. Afra works in front of it: the people, the occasions, what the map becomes in somebody else's hands.

It grew under pressure

Almost the entire collection was made during the two years I was working on biennales — thirty-seven recordings in April, sixteen in May, eighty-eight in June, one of the heaviest production months of my working life. The slowest months produced the least.

The archive already here, and the field around it

An Urban Archive of District 4 has run since 2016; Andrew Stiff's Moving Experiences (2021) and Peripheral Poetics (2026), Thierry Bernard's More-Than-Noise (2026) and Rachel Jahja's motorbike paper (2025) work the same city. Every one of them instruments capture — the lens, the mount, the interval, the route. The wider family is older than the school: Stalker walking Rome's edges from the mid-1990s, and the counter-mapping and community-archive practices that treat a record of a place as something people make rather than something taken from them.

04 — Meeting through doing with

Meeting through doing with — becoming part of a place rather than reporting on one.

Four principles, and none of them is a procedure. Each one is falsifiable, and two of them the practice has already failed.

Make the room,
not the content

Fix the format; leave the material to other people.

In 2022 the architects working on the tube house were not in touch with each other, even though five of the six had once been classmates. I think Vietnamese contemporary architecture was, at that point, a set of serious practices operating in isolation, each with a following and almost no relation to the others. There was no room in which they were all present.

So we made the room. That is the whole of the intervention: a date, a venue, an invitation, a subject narrow enough that everyone present was already an expert in it, and a format that required people to travel between each other's studios rather than sit in rows. We printed something closer to a treasure map than a programme.

I had no expertise to offer. I was a foreigner with no architectural training, in a city I had lived in for months. What I had was the ability to make a room in which people who did know would talk to each other in public.

Publish the rule

A frame is only accountable if its terms can be read.

The Gặp Series is where I have not written the frame down. Since 2023 we have been going to see practitioners whose way of working leaves room for slowness and reflection: architects, a designer making furniture from objects found on the street, an anthropologist of Vietnamese urbanism. The format is deliberately loose — five to fifteen people, no rigid structure, participants becoming the interviewers — because the point is the curiosity, not the protocol.

And the records stay unpublished. Every session was recorded somehow — audio, photographs — but no date, no venue, no guest list is published anywhere. What is missing is not the record and not a rule. It is the public, checkable account of the community this was assembling.

Publishing the rule converts a good intention into something that can be checked by someone other than me — and the failure here is not an admin oversight. A community of practice that exists only in my memory is not evidence.

Keep the hold short

Short cycles are held well. Long ones drift.

Holding a frame for an evening is hosting. Holding one for four years is editing, and editing means deciding on other people's behalf, repeatedly, in ways they can contest. These are different operations and I have only learned the first.

The evidence is in the formats. Three zines in a year is a rhythm. One book in four years is a stall. The Reinterpreting Narrowness book is something we attempted, and it never saw the light: we were chasing parameters of perfection and overcuration that do not exist.

Holding briefly explains a pattern I had been reading as a personal failure to finish things. It is more specific than that: this practice holds frames whose terms are published up front and whose holding period is short.

Withdraw

The frame is proven by what continues without you.

Reinterpreting Narrowness began as five practices and one evening. It is now twenty-five practices, and they went on meeting without us. We did not convene the later gatherings and were not always told about them. The community that formed inside our frame outlived our holding of it, and detached.

Meike Schalk and Apolonija Sustersic built a garden in an Edinburgh close for the 2007 exhibition Jardins Publics, calling their own role "intermediaries in a process of self-organisation". When the exhibition closed the residents kept the garden.

What continues without me is the only evidence a practice like this can offer. Not that people enjoyed it. Not that eighty people came. That the thing continued in our absence and stopped requiring us.

TUAR — Tactical Urbanism at Turtle Lake, 2023. Fifteen children aged four and five, from Little Em's preschool, observing and reimagining Hồ Con Rùa. They read the site from the inside out: the middle was the place, and the traffic was weather. It is the inverse of how every plan of that location is drawn, and it is the only project where my frame was the only frame in the room.

05 — Two ways of not knowing

Two relations to not-knowing, and I have not decided whether they can coexist.

Between August 2025 and August 2026 I took leave from the candidature to direct art production for the Islamic Arts Biennale 2025 in Jeddah, and to manage production for the Bukhara Biennial 2025 and the Diriyah Contemporary Art Biennale 2026.

In the practice, not-knowing is the condition you stay inside long enough for something to arrive. In production, not-knowing is a risk with a cost and a date attached, and the entire craft consists of eliminating it early. Both are forms of professional seriousness. They are not compatible on the same afternoon.

These are two non-overlapping communities of practice with different standards for good work and different economies of recognition. I belong to both. What earns respect in one is close to invisible in the other.

The leave cost the schedule twelve months and gave the research its comparative structure.

06 — What continues, and what that proves

Continuation after withdrawal is the evidence — and on its own it proves nothing.

Not did it continue but what powers transferred.

Continuation is my main evidence, and on its own it proves nothing. The architects I first invited had been at university together — though they had not been meeting for years, so what continued may be an old network reactivating rather than anything my frame did. What separates the two is not whether the group kept going but whether it changed: how the format, who comes, and what they now do differ from what we set up in October 2022. That I can go and find out. On the harder question, whether any real power moved, none has yet.

The Rare Sea Gallery monthly workshops, from October 2026, are the first case where that transfer has been designed in advance rather than found afterwards.

What this does not prove. As of Milestone 2, nothing has yet been amended without me. And the Gặp Series is three years old and every session was recorded somehow — but nothing is published: no date, no venue, no guest list anywhere public.

07 — Holding the frame

The method, arrived at rather than assumed — and the exhibition built as a frame.

Ho Chi Minh City, late 2028. Not a retrospective.

  • The archive is audible in the room, unmixed and unsequenced, at whatever length each recording was made — the opposite of the 2022 dark room, and shown against it. Maybe not. I think I want to bring a mastered one.
  • The examination itself might have a guest, and might become a Gặp Series session.
  • The archive credited as a whole rather than by name, which is how it was built: the instrument has never asked anybody who they are.
  • The Reinterpreting Narrowness community is invited to use the space for one of its own gatherings, which we do not programme, and I don't want to be part of it.

Scheduled deliberately before the writing concludes: the exhibition is not documentation of a finished argument but the last experiment the argument needs.
