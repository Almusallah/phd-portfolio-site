A Practice of Encounter
with Ho Chi Minh City

Reading and sharing its intangible layer: night, sound, light, sidewalks, movement, temporary occupation and the small changes through which the same street becomes a different place across the day.

Yuri Frassis4017499 · DR207
Officine Gặpwith Afra Rebuscini, est. 2022

01 — Reading and sharing the intangible layer

The qualities of this city available to perception are shaped by the relation between a place, the person encountering it, and the means through which that encounter is attended to, recorded or shared.

The research examines a practice of encounter with Ho Chi Minh City. It works from the proposition that the qualities of the city available to perception are shaped by the relation between a place, the person encountering it and the means through which that encounter is attended to, recorded or shared.

Intangible layer is a working name for shifting sensory and social conditions: sound without clear edges, light that changes the spatial reading of a street, sidewalks moving between circulation, work and social use, and the different tempo of the city after dark. The term does not mean immaterial. It identifies qualities that are relational, temporal and difficult to fix in a plan.

Framework of encounter is the broad term for an arrangement that shapes an encounter. Instrument is reserved for a device or format that somebody can operate, most clearly the Saigon_Miền Tây Sound Map. A walk, a dark listening room, a public archive and a convened discussion establish different conditions and distribute decisions differently.

Walking has been part of the practice since 2019. It is loose, generally after six in the evening, with no fixed route, duration or task. Returning to familiar ground makes change perceptible and produces photographs, recordings and notes when something catches my attention. Attentiveness describes a recurring condition in my own practice; it is not an outcome I can attribute to another person from an upload.

The sound map gives this research its clearest public instrument. It allows somebody else to record, locate and share a fragment from their own position. Its interface, categories, upload path and technical infrastructure can be examined directly, while the archive also shows the limits of the present evidence: it records submissions better than it records the experience of contributing.

02 — Understanding through doing

The practice develops through repeated encounters, recording, collaboration, production and convening.

These activities recur across the work without forming a single protocol.

Walking

Arriving on foot, at length, and usually late.

Walking has been part of the practice since 2019, first in Da Nang and later here. Usually after dark, generally after six. There is no fixed route, no set duration and nothing I have to come back with. Returning to the same ground makes change perceptible without turning the walk into a fixed route.

Further back, in Rome in the 1990s, a group called Stalker made walking the city their primary method: collective, unhurried, attentive to the parts of a city that planning disregards. Francesco Careri and Lorenzo Romito describe their own role at Campo Boario as producing neither objects nor projects, only paths and relationships. That is the family I belong to, practices that treat being in a city, at length and on foot, as a legitimate way of producing knowledge. What does not transfer is the ground. They walked ground that had been let go of; I walk ground in full use, by people who are working.

What walking makes available. It creates the possibility of encountering material I did not go looking for.

Recording

Brief recordings of passing conditions.

The recordings are short. They are not field recordings in the ethnographic sense; nobody is documenting an hour of a market to capture its structure. A recording catches a passing condition rather than characterising a place.

What a glimpse can and cannot hold. It records what stopped me, which is a fact about attention rather than about the city. A glimpse does not tell you everything about the city. It tells you what caught your attention at a particular moment.

Producing

Making a lot, together, and in public.

Between 2022 and 2025, Afra and I sustained a sequence of public work: two sound exhibitions in two cities inside three weeks; an architecture week; a workshop with five-year-olds that ended in an exhibition; several sound walks and zines, then the Vietnamese editions; talks in Saigon, Bangkok and Trento; and the map. Alongside all the conversations and studio visits of the Gặp Series. Short cycles, published, then the next one.

A current limit. The Reinterpreting Narrowness publication remains in progress, while recordings, photographs and notes from other projects also remain unpublished. The practice has produced material faster than two people can edit and publish it. Resolving the publication and documenting the Gặp Series are part of the next research phase.

Earning trust

Slowly, in person, before anything is asked.

The architects opened their studios after a period of visits and conversation. Little Em's involved fifteen children in TUAR. The Blind Associations contributed to the development of Crafting A Sonic City, and practitioners have hosted Gặp Series conversations in their working spaces. These relationships shape who can be invited, what can be discussed and how a public encounter is held.

What this requires. Invitation and hospitality are authored conditions. They depend on continuing relationships and cannot be treated as a neutral procedure.

03 — The instrument

An instrument built from my own way of moving through the city, which somebody else can operate on an ordinary phone.

Saigon_Miền Tây Sound Map, 2025– · 259 recordings as at 31 August 2026 · open contribution.

The live public archive. Open it in its own window ↗

Crafting A Sonic City, 2022, and the sound map establish two different conditions of listening. The dark room was temporary, controlled and witnessed. The map persists online, is operated remotely and is usually encountered without me present. They belong to the same research line, but the map was not designed as a single solution to a failure of the earlier project. It developed from a continuing concern with duration, access and contribution alongside practical and technical decisions.

What is authored here is the instrument: the map itself, the categories, and the fact that anybody can open it and add to it. Publishing it puts those decisions where they can be examined. The recordings show that it accumulates and is used; they do not by themselves show what anyone noticed.

How it actually works

What I decide is how the instrument is built. Each recording is a pin at the coordinates where it was made. The player gives title, category, date, duration, audio and, since July 2026, an optional photograph. There is no waveform, which would make a short glimpse look like a specimen.

What happens after that is not mine. Anyone can open the map and record into it. Nothing is filtered and nothing waits for me: an upload goes straight onto the map. I occasionally record into it myself, like anybody else. The only power I keep afterwards is deletion, which I have not had to use.

And the instrument does not record who contributed. There is no name field on the upload form, and the database does not record contributor identity. When I built it I did not consider adding an author field, because my attention was on what was uploaded rather than who uploaded it. I still value that emphasis on the recording, but the missing field now limits what I can establish about contribution and authorship from the database alone.

Ethics and review. Ethics approval for the research has been obtained. A periodic review of new uploads with a Vietnamese-speaking reviewer is planned, focused on potentially identifying spoken information. That safeguard has not operated to date. Location is not screened: every recording is published at the coordinates where it was made, which is a decision built into the instrument.

The field this sits in

Public audiovisual archiving of this city did not begin here. An Urban Archive of District 4, collated by Andrew Stiff and four research colleagues, is public, categorised and contributable, and nine years older than this archive. Thierry Bernard's More-Than-Noise is a listening practice in the same city, and the Vietnam Sound Library collects much of the same material to different ends. Open public sound mapping is long established internationally: radio aporee has taken uploads from anyone, without a login, since 2006, and Cities and Memory holds more than eight thousand sounds from over two thousand five hundred contributors.

What this research adds is narrower. It turns a situated way of encountering Ho Chi Minh City into an instrument a non-specialist can operate on an ordinary phone, and makes the authored conditions of that instrument, the categories, the refusals, the upload path, available for examination.

What it is made of

It is six services, each doing one small job. Your phone's browser is the recorder — there is no app to install and no account to make. Render is the computer that receives what you send. Backblaze is the shelf where the sound files sit. MongoDB is the notebook that says what each file is and where it was made. Mapbox draws the map you see. And GitHub keeps the code, plus a copy of the notebook every week.

Five of those are free. We pay for one, the computer that receives your recordings so that everyone can upload at the same time and the site never falls asleep.

What happens when you press upload. The sound goes to the computer, the computer converts it and puts the file on the shelf, then writes one line in the notebook — title, category, date, the point on the map, how long it is. The map reads the notebook and draws your dot. About ten seconds.

About the sound itself. Your phone measures the air 48,000 times a second. We keep 44,100 of those measurements. That is the same as a CD, and it covers everything a human ear can hear — more than that only helps if you want to slow the sound down later. We save it as MP3, at a generous quality so that converting it doesn't damage it. One minute is about one and a half megabytes, small enough to open on phone data. We use MP3 because it plays on everything — iPhones cannot play the format phones record in, so without converting, every iPhone would hear silence.

One choice matters more than all of that. A phone assumes you are on a call. It tries to remove everything that is not a voice close to the microphone. We turned that off. Otherwise the phone would delete exactly what we came for — the background, the distance, the quiet.

Your data. No accounts, no tracking, nothing personal. If you add a photo, we delete the hidden location information inside it before saving. Every sound file is named with its own coordinates, so even if the notebook were lost, the map could be rebuilt from the file names. And the whole archive can be downloaded by anyone who wants to work with it.

Its live present

The map enters Nổ Cái Bùm with a zine and a sound performance by Vũ, the sound practitioner involved in the programme. Monthly sound walks and workshops with Rare Sea Gallery are planned from October.

Building and activating the instrument

Most of my hours go into building the thing itself rather than recording: the software, what a recording is allowed to carry, who can contribute, how it gets activated.

The technical work includes recording behaviour, interface decisions, access, storage and maintenance. Those decisions shape what the archive can retain and who can use it.

Officine Gặp is a shared practice with Afra Rebuscini. I have spent much of the project time on the underlying system; Afra has often worked more directly on activation, people and the circumstances in which the archive becomes a public programme. The roles overlap, and shared projects remain co-authored.

What the archive count establishes

On 31 August 2026 the live interface displayed 259 recordings. The figure establishes accumulation and public use. It does not identify distinct contributors, make the archive representative of the city or demonstrate a change in perception.

The archive already here, and the field around it

An Urban Archive of District 4 whose fieldwork began in September 2016, has run since then; Andrew Stiff's Moving experiences (2020) and Peripheral Poetics: Trip 1–5 (2026), Thierry Bernard's More-Than-Noise (2026) work in the same city. Each of them is an instrument of capture — the lens, the mount, the interval, the route. The wider family is older than the school: Stalker walking Rome's edges from the mid-1990s, and the counter-mapping and community-archive practices that treat a record of a place as something people make rather than something taken from them.

04 — Convening the city

Convening makes invitation, setting, duration and the conditions of meeting part of the research.

The formats distribute authorship without making the organiser disappear.

Convening
the room

Invitation and format establish the conditions of meeting.

Reinterpreting Narrowness began in 2022 around architecture practices working on the tube house. Six studios were invited and five opened their studios during an architecture week that also included a panel and workshops.

Officine Gặp selected the subject, issued the invitations, set the schedule and established the public format. Practitioners contributed the knowledge and material of the programme. The room was therefore authored even though its content was not ours alone.

Choosing who to invite changes the field before an event begins. The research examines that selection, the conditions of hospitality and what continues after the immediate encounter ends.

Duration and
editorial responsibility

Short public formats and long publication cycles require different forms of responsibility.

Convening an evening and editing a multi-year publication are different operations. The first establishes a temporary setting; the second requires repeated decisions on behalf of contributors and a durable editorial structure.

The City Otherwise produced three issues in 2025. The Reinterpreting Narrowness publication remains in progress and is planned to bring together work from twenty-five practices.

The practice has worked more effectively through short public formats than through long editorial processes. The publication backlog is a practical and research limit that the completion plan addresses directly.

What continues

Activity after the original event and the limits of that evidence.

Reinterpreting Narrowness began in 2022 with an architecture week: a panel, workshops and two days of open studios. Six practices were invited and five opened their doors. Later meetings and activity continued after Officine Gặp stopped convening every encounter.

The in-progress publication is planned to bring together work from twenty-five practices. That wider participation shows continuation, but the network also has a history that predates my intervention.

Continuation is evidence of consequence and persistence. It does not by itself establish that authority transferred. Chapter 6 will examine who now convenes, who decides, what Officine Gặp still controls and what it no longer controls.

TUAR — Tactical Urbanism at Turtle Lake, 2023. Fifteen children aged four and five, from Little Em's preschool, observing and reimagining Hồ Con Rùa. They read the site from the inside out: the middle was the place, and the traffic was weather. It is the inverse of how every plan of that location is drawn, and it is the only project where my frame was the only frame in the room.

05 — Producing other people's work

Biennale production introduced a methodological contrast in delegation, responsibility and not-knowing.

Between August 2025 and August 2026, I took a leave of absence from my candidature to work as art production manager on the Islamic Arts Biennale 2025 in Jeddah and the Bukhara Biennial 2025, and to direct the whole art production department for the Diriyah Contemporary Art Biennale 2026.

Large-scale art production required consequential decisions to be delegated while I remained responsible for the whole. Uncertainty had to be identified early, assigned and managed against budgets, dependencies and fixed opening dates.

This professional work is not evidence about Ho Chi Minh City. It provides a contrast that sharpens a question already present in the sound map and the convening work: how authorship and responsibility persist when control is distributed.

The comparison makes delegation concrete. In the doctoral cases, the research can examine which decisions remain with the author, which are made by participants or collaborators, and what can no longer be seen once a framework is handed over.

06 — Continuation and transfer of authority

Continuation supports a claim of persistence; transfer of authority requires different evidence.

The next phase documents who convenes, who decides and what changes after the original event.

Activity continued after the 2022 event, but five of the six studios initially invited had studied together, so the network predates my intervention. Continuation demonstrates consequence and persistence. It does not by itself show that authority transferred. That stronger claim requires evidence of changes to the format, invitations or decisions that were not made by Officine Gặp.

Planned Rare Sea Gallery workshops create situations in which participants can use the sound map while discussing or contesting its categories and conditions. Their outcomes are not part of the present evidence.

Present limit. The existing database and record of events do not yet establish a transfer of authority. The next phase will document decisions, disagreements and changes through which that question can be assessed.

07 — Exhibition experiment and completion

The exhibition is planned as a research experiment bringing the encounter frameworks into one setting.

Ho Chi Minh City, late 2027 or early 2028.

  • Compare the sound map and listening-room interface as two arrangements of the same archive.
  • Bring walking, listening, public contribution and convening into a shared setting.
  • Document how participants use, discuss, alter or refuse the frameworks.
  • Use the resulting evidence in Chapters 6 and 7 and the final synthesis.

The design remains to be developed. The present milestone establishes the research question and completion sequence, not a fixed exhibition protocol.

The two tracks. The Hanoi and Saigon compositions from the 2022 dark rooms, with HUM Studios. Every decision in them was ours — which is what the 2027 room gives up.
