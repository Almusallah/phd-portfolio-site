01 — Never quite dark

Reality is an artifact our perception creates.

I mean that plainly. The city I walk around in is not a thing sitting there waiting to be recorded accurately. It is something produced, continuously, in the encounter between a place and an apparatus — a body, a habit, a camera, a category, a language. Change the apparatus and you get a different city, and neither version is the false one.

That is not a retreat into subjectivity. It is the opposite. An impression cannot be examined. An apparatus can: it can be built, published, handed to somebody else and argued with. So if the apparatus is what does the making, the apparatus is the thing to examine — and that is a research method rather than a confession. It is also why the sections below are organised around what the practice does rather than what it has made.

And the city gets its share. If perception makes the city, does the place contribute anything — or is this idealism with a camera? The load-bearing word is encounter, and an encounter has two sides. What the apparatus does is not invent. It selects, from a place already denser than any apparatus can take, and that surplus is not an inconvenience: it is the evidence the place is there, doing its own part. I know it is there by the resistance it puts up — recordings arrive that no category fits, categories fill with things I did not predict, a street refuses to sound the way the scheme says it should, and the scheme is what has to move.

The word apparatus means anything standing between a place and a perception of it, shaping what comes through: a body, a habit, a lens, a category list, a language. On this account the difference between a good and a bad instrument is not accuracy but whether it is legible enough to be worked against. A design criterion rather than a philosophical one, which is why it can be examined rather than only argued about.

Four conditions in this work make the apparatus visible, and I have worked in all of them. The night, when attention reorganises and things that were there all along surface because the volume of everything else has dropped. The removed sense — in 2022 we sat people in a dark room with their eyes covered and played them the city; that is usually called a sound project, and it was not, it was an experiment on perception. The photographic frame, since every photograph is an argument about where the edges are and the twelve series of Project Vietnam are twelve categories I invented and then went looking for. And classification itself: the eighteen categories of the sound map are the same operation made explicit and made public.

The night is the first of four, not the frame around the other three. I return to it most because it is where I started and where I am still most often out. A portfolio that made it the whole subject would be describing my habits rather than my argument.

Stalker and Francesco Careri are the closest precedent for the method: Careri writes that the walk builds nothing and yet transforms a place and its meanings, and teaches a course conducted entirely by walking, getting lost and wasting time. What does not transfer is the ground. Stalker walked terrain vague — peripheries, largely abandoned. What I walk is the opposite: dense, occupied, in constant use, a street life that is nobody's leftover. A walk through an emptied periphery answers to nobody; a walk down a working sidewalk answers to everyone on it. Re-aiming the method at occupied ground is mine to do.

The literature theorises this territory and never builds anything for it. The Situationists make ambiance researchable from 1955 and rule out the last hours of the night. Careri and Stalker carry walking into the terrain vague, by day. Nick Dunn makes the night his object and names the shift away from the eye — then has no apparatus for it, and treats the nocturnal city as a place that rewards attention rather than as evidence that attention makes cities. None of them treats the apparatus itself as the authored work. That is where this practice goes.

This portfolio is ordered to the structure of the dissertation rather than chronologically, so the two can be read against each other and the structure tested. The claim comes first and the method comes last: you arrive at the frame, you do not start there. Each move states what it carries, the projects that carry it, and what it costs. The organising principle of sections 02 and 04 — two movements, each named by technique rather than by project — is a debt to Géraldine Borio's Parallel Works (RMIT, 2020), and to the principle only.

02 — A way to discover

Understanding through doing — how a practice comes to know a place it does not belong to.

Five techniques. The projects appear across all of them, doing different work in each.

Walking

Arriving on foot, at length, and usually late.

The same procedure in every city I have lived in: arrive somewhere I do not know, walk it at length, photograph what stops me, find the people who seem to be doing something interesting. Only then make anything. I did not recognise it as a procedure until recently.

What it costs. It is the only technique that produces material I did not go looking for.

  • Trà đá Party · Dạo Nghe, Oct 2022

Recording

Taking the glimpse rather than the survey.

The median recording on the sound map is thirty-six seconds. Seventy-eight per cent are under a minute. These are not field recordings in the ethnographic sense; nobody is documenting an hour of a market to capture its structure. The archive is made of attention spans.

That has a cost worth naming. Thirty-six seconds establishes a scene and cannot establish a process. You cannot hear a transaction complete or a street empty.

What it costs. It records what stopped me, which is a fact about attention rather than about the city.

  • CASC — 78 Saigon, 74 Hanoi
  • Sound map — 157 recordings, 2h12m

Classifying

Declaring in advance what a place is made of.

Eighteen categories, written by me, published where anyone can read them. They are social: they describe what people are doing. Sidewalks, Street Vendors, Repairing and Work, Ritual and Ceremony.

On 6 May 2026 I deleted the residual category — the Other bin — and made the field mandatory. Bowker and Star treat the residual bin as the most revealing part of any classification, because it is where a system deposits what it declines to think about. Removing it forces the scheme to claim completeness, which is a claim about the city rather than a decision about a dropdown menu.

What it costs. The scheme is the authored object. Not the recordings. Six years earlier I had already done it without noticing: Project Vietnam's twelve series names are a classification I invented and then went looking for.

  • Project Vietnam — 12 series
  • Sound map — 18 categories
  • Residual category deleted, 6 May 2026

Titling

Naming in the language you are standing inside.

There is a recording on the map that I called gâu gâu. It is a dog, barking, in an alley in District 4. In English I would have written "woof", and it would have been a different animal.

I did not decide to title it in Vietnamese. Months later, going through the archive as a whole, I found that forty-two of the hundred and fifty-seven recordings are titled that way; not translated, titled. Quét vỉa hè, sweeping the sidewalk. Dừng đèn đỏ, stopped at a red light. They cluster in Sidewalks and Street Vendors: the categories where the sound is most obviously somebody's work.

What it costs. It is not a technique. The frame recorded my position without being asked to, which is a warning about everything else in it.

  • 42 of 157 recordings titled in Vietnamese

Withholding

Refusing a category, and publishing the refusal.

There is no category called Noise. A taxonomy with a Noise bin would have sorted some of this city's residents into it — not sounds, people. The vendor's call, the karaoke, the horn, the ceremony: the sounds classified as noise here are overwhelmingly the sounds of somebody working or worshipping in public. The category would not have been a neutral bin. It would have been continuous with an existing apparatus of removal.

And there is a category that is empty. Announcements and Signals, icon a loudspeaker, live since 6 May 2026, holding nothing. Behind it, disabled in the code, sit a hundred and ninety lines of sourced writing on rao hàng and loa phường that I wrote, published into the page, and switched off on 19 May 2026 under the commit message "hide Heritage feature".

Four lines lower in the same file, the three audio processes browsers apply by default — echo cancellation, noise suppression, automatic gain control — are all turned off. They exist to identify the city as interference and subtract it.

What it costs. It is the clearest evidence that the authorship here lives in the scheme. If it lived in the recordings, an empty category with a suppressed essay behind it would be a bug rather than a legible act.

  • Announcements & Signals — empty
  • Heritage module — written, hidden
  • 3 audio filters disabled

The closed version. In 2022 visitors sat in a dark room with their eyes covered and received eight minutes we had composed. Every decision was already made. Nothing in the encounter allowed anyone to disagree with our reading of the city, because our reading was the only thing in the room. The sound map is the same project with the authorship moved out of the composition and into the frame.

03 — The instrument

The instrument is the authored work. Not the recordings it holds, and not any one state of its classification.

Saigon_Miền Tây Sound Map, 2025– · 157 recordings · 18 categories · open contribution. One section of this portfolio is given to a single work, and the exception is deliberate: a claim that the classification is the authored thing is obliged to put the artefact on the table and let it be examined.

It began four years before it existed, in a dark room. Crafting A Sonic City, 2022: an audience with their eyes covered, played the city. That project worked and then it was over. An evening in a dark room is a very efficient way of making an argument and a very inefficient way of keeping one. The recordings went back onto a hard drive. Nothing had been made available to anybody who was not in the room.

Everything technical about the map answers that one problem: how do you make an atmosphere available to someone who was not there?

The recordings are evidence. Any competent person with a recorder and enough evenings could make them. What cannot be arrived at by diligence is the decision about what this city is made of — which is what a category scheme is. To publish eighteen categories is to make a claim, in public, in advance, about the constituents of a place. Everything that arrives afterwards is either accommodated by that claim or strains against it, and the strain is data.

The scheme, and where it came from

In the order they are authored in — not alphabetical, and the order is itself a decision: Food & Drink · Sidewalks · Street Vendors · Music · Home · Work · Repairing · Conversations · Vehicles · Markets · Shops · Play & Leisure · Ritual & Ceremony · Announcements & Signals · Infrastructure & Utilities · Nature · Waterways · Background.

The list is hard-coded in two files, client and server, so the dropdown a contributor is offered and the filter bar cannot drift apart — and so adding a category is a git commit. That is why the intellectual history of this scheme is recoverable at all. Largest by count: Sidewalks 25, Music 21, Conversations 17, Background 14, Street Vendors 13. Forty-two of 157 recordings carry Vietnamese titles — 27% overall, but nine of thirteen in Street Vendors.

And the scheme has a documented turning point. On 6 May 2026, in one commit: the residual category Others was deleted, surviving only as an alias to Background, which became the new default; and Announcements & Signals was added. Category is a required field, so nothing arrives uncategorised. Deleting the residual bin removes the place where anything unclassifiable could quietly go — a scheme without an Others has to be right, or be changed — and the category added in its place has held zero recordings ever since. An author-invented slot with no occupant, offered publicly, waiting for material that has not come. The emptiest thing in the archive and the loudest evidence that the categories were authored rather than derived.

How they were derived, since this is the question the scheme has to survive. Not inductively from a corpus and not deductively from a theory: authored in advance, out of five years of walking and photographing this city, then corrected by what arrived. A weaker-sounding origin than either alternative, and the honest one — the first list was a proposition about what Ho Chi Minh City is made of, written by someone who had been paying attention and had no authority to write it.

Eighteen is not derived from anything. It is where the scheme stands after a sequence of dated decisions. A scheme whose number was derived — from a theory, a survey, an existing taxonomy — would be claiming a warrant it does not have. What is defensible is not the number but the trail: every addition, merge and refusal carries a date and a reason, and that is what makes it an examinable artefact rather than a personal preference presented as a structure.

Three decisions that carry the argument

Sidewalks is the largest category. Not traffic, which everyone predicts; not markets, which the tourist literature predicts. A scheme that let the pavement be a subheading of street would have been a scheme that could not see its own subject.

The median recording is thirty-six seconds. A position, not a limit. The unit of this archive is a glimpse rather than a survey.

There is no Noise category, and the absence is published. To open one is to accept the whole apparatus that treats urban sound as nuisance — the decibel map, the complaint, the ordinance. That apparatus exists and it is not stupid. It is not what this instrument is for. The refusal is a refusal to let the intangible layer be entered in a ledger where its only available value is negative.

How it actually works

And the base is wrong. The map sits on an ordinary street map, while everything I argue says the street grid is not this city's ground — what I record is alleys, thresholds and times of day. Whether redrawing that base is a distraction or this project's most honest drawing is an open question I carry into the milestone.

And a limit I would rather state than have found. The categories are titled in Vietnamese, but they were not thought in Vietnamese — they were authored in English and translated, and have not been back-translated or tested with native speakers as a scheme. Until that is done, the Vietnamese titling is a courtesy at the surface of an apparatus authored elsewhere, and I should not describe it as more than that.

Each recording is a pin at the coordinates where it was made; category determines the pin. The visual density of a district is a direct read-out of where I have been — an honest map of my own range rather than of the city's. The player gives title, category, date, duration and the audio, and deliberately no waveform: a waveform invites the measuring attention this archive exists to hold off, and makes a thirty-six-second glimpse look like a specimen. Titles are in Vietnamese, which means every one passes through somebody else — a title I cannot write alone is a title I cannot quietly load.

Submissions enter a queue rather than the map, because the category decision on an incoming recording is the exact point where the authored scheme meets somebody else's ear. Three ways a submission fails to fit: misuse, which is my badly written label; genuine ambiguity, a sidewalk that is also a workshop, which here is most sidewalks; and the one that matters — a category I do not have. Two of the current eighteen arrived that way.

A published failure. The archive originally served WebM — smaller, technically better, and inaudible on Safari on iOS. For a period a public archive of a Southeast Asian city could not be heard on the most common way of getting online in that city. Migrated to MP3, June 2026. The format decision was a distribution decision wearing technical clothes, and I did not see it until somebody could not hear it.

Its live present

The map is not waiting for 2028. It enters Nổ Cái Bùm with a zine and a sound performance by Vu, a sound practitioner building his own modular synthesiser — and the person who told me I had stopped making recordings and started making an instrument. From October, a collaboration with Rare Sea Gallery: monthly sound walks and workshops. A monthly room, on a schedule, with recorders in it, is the only place where eighteen categories can be argued with by somebody other than me.

The instrument is the work, and it is not mine alone

Most of my hours do not go into recording. They go into building and improving the thing itself: the software, the categories, what a recording is allowed to carry, who can contribute, how it gets activated. Recordings can now hold a photograph alongside the audio, and deciding that took longer than making a hundred recordings. Vu — who stopped making music with instruments and started building the instrument — is the person who pointed out that I had done the same without noticing. The recordings are what the work produces. The instrument is the work. That sentence came from a conversation, not from me.

I build it daily, in conversation with an AI, the way Vu solders. I state that plainly because an argument that the apparatus must be published cannot then decline to publish how the apparatus was made.

And the instrument does not work when it sits. The map does its real work when Afra builds activation events around it — when it is staged, played, walked with. It is not an archive that rests; it is an instrument that has to be performed, and performing it is not my skill. Officine Gặp names a two-person operation in which the building and the activating are different jobs held by different people.

It grew under pressure

One fact I did not expect and cannot explain away. Almost the entire collection was made during the two years I was working on biennales — thirty-seven recordings in April, sixteen in May, eighty-eight in June — and that June was one of the heaviest production months of my working life. The slowest, least instrumental thing I do was at its most active in the months I was most scheduled. I think the recording, and the building, may have been how I metabolised the other work — which means the two ways of working I spent two years treating as alternatives were running simultaneously the whole time, and I did not notice.

The archive already here, and the field around it

An Urban Archive of District 4, Ho Chi Minh City has been running since September 2016 — public, browsable, holding video, photography, audio, drawing and mixed reality from the hẻms of Ward 14, over four collections and an open tag vocabulary. A public, categorised, contributable audiovisual archive of a district of this city, made nine years before mine. Any account of what the sound map contributes has to begin there.

It states its own position clearly, and it is not mine: "our archive is not so much a documentary of the hẻms, but one that responds to what we look at in the studio." Both of us are making an apparatus argument, at opposite ends of the pipeline. Their creative act happens after capture, and their classification is a retrieval layer. Mine happens before capture — the categories were published in advance of most of the recordings now in them, they are closed rather than additive, dated, and their refusals are published alongside their contents. Nothing here is processed at all.

Nor does it stand alone. Andrew Stiff's Moving Experiences: Video Observations of Ho Chi Minh City (2021) films the hẻms and runs the footage through customised lenses and filters; his Peripheral Poetics trips (2026) do it from a motorbike. Thierry Bernard's More-Than-Noise: Listening with Saigon Soundscapes (2026) is a listening practice in this city finishing now. Stiff, Jahja, Bernard & Youn's Interdisciplinary Weavings (2026) takes the Kênh Tẻ canal into twenty-eight metres of film, environmental sound and cyanotype. Rachel Jahja's motorbike paper (2025) reports nine traverses of all nineteen districts, camera fixed behind the driver, screengrabs every five to ten seconds.

Put beside each other, these are all apparatus arguments, and every one of them instruments capture — the lens, the filter, the mount, the interval, the route. None of them classifies. So the local field has thoroughly established that the instrument of capture is authorable, and has left the instrument of sorting almost untouched. That is a narrower claim to novelty than I would once have made and a much more defensible one, and it only becomes visible when the work next door is named.

The objection, given at full strength

My defence has been a split: the categories are mine, the collecting is theirs. That is Ida Halpern's position, restated almost word for word. Halpern recorded Kwakwaka'wakw songs across decades and was scrupulous about naming her singers. Once the recordings entered archival collections — as Dylan Robinson records in Hungry Listening — "Halpern is the only individual whose artistry is recognized." Intent did not enter into it.

Much of Robinson does not transfer: his argument runs on a seventy-year potlatch ban and on ownership by named hereditary rights holders, and there is no hereditary rights holder for a street. But strip the settler-colonial frame out entirely and three claims stand: the taxonomy is authored power; the collector accrues the attribution whatever the collector intends; and access-is-good is a premise, not a fact.

The answer is not a paragraph of acknowledgement — Robinson calls that a non-performative utterance. Three structural changes, dated: guest listening as the declared position, his term, not mine; a present-but-withheld state in the archive, so the instrument contains a page that says stop here; and a prescriptive score naming which categories carry protocol weight, who holds editorial authority over them, and how material can be reclassified or removed by whoever contributed it.

What this does not prove. Not that the categories are correct — correctness is not available; a scheme is a proposition about a city, judged by what it lets you see and what it costs you. Not that the archive is representative; the pin density says otherwise. And not that the transfer of authority above has occurred. As of this submission it is designed, partly built, and scheduled.

04 — A way to make with

Meeting through doing with — becoming part of a place rather than reporting on one.

Five principles. Values rather than a method — but each one is falsifiable, and three of them the practice has already failed.

Make the room,
not the content

Fix the format; leave the material to other people.

In 2022 the architects working on the tube house did not know each other. Vietnamese contemporary architecture was a set of serious practices operating in isolation, each with a following and almost no relation to the others. There was no room in which they were all present.

So we made the room. That is the whole of the intervention: a date, a venue, an invitation, a subject narrow enough that everyone present was already an expert in it, and a format that required people to travel between each other's studios rather than sit in rows. We printed something closer to a treasure map than a programme.

I value this principle because I had no expertise to offer. I was a foreigner with no architectural training, in a city I had lived in for months. What I had was the ability to make a room in which people who did know would talk to each other in public.

Publish the rule

A frame is only accountable if its terms can be read.

Jo Freeman's argument holds here and it is the best defence this practice has: there is no structureless group, only groups whose structure is unacknowledged and therefore unaccountable. An explicit, published, amendable frame is more democratic than a facilitator's tacit charisma.

But it obliges me to write the frame down, and the Gặp Series is where I have not. Since 2023 we have been going to see practitioners whose way of working leaves room for slowness and reflection: architects, a designer making furniture from objects found on the street, an anthropologist of Vietnamese urbanism. The format is deliberately loose — five to fifteen people, no rigid structure, participants becoming the interviewers — because the point is the curiosity, not the protocol.

And not one session has a public record. No date, no venue, no guest list published anywhere. What is missing is not a rule. It is any account at all of the community this was assembling, which is the thing the panel has been asking me to name since Confirmation.

I value this principle because it converts a good intention into something that can be checked by someone other than me — and because the failure here is not an admin oversight. A community of practice that exists only in my memory is not evidence.

Keep the hold short

Short cycles are held well. Long ones drift.

Holding a frame for an evening is hosting. Holding one for four years is editing, and editing means deciding on other people's behalf, repeatedly, in ways they can contest. These are different operations and I have only learned the first.

The evidence is in the formats. Three zines in a year is a rhythm. One book in four years is a stall. The Reinterpreting Narrowness book was proposed on 19 September 2023 — twenty-three built projects, three commissioned essays, all contributors confirmed, a hundred and fifty to two hundred pages. It has not been published.

I value this principle because it explains a pattern I had been reading as a personal failure to finish things. It is more specific than that: this practice holds frames whose terms are published up front and whose holding period is short.

Name everyone

Credit is structural, not courtesy.

Three failures, and none of them was a decision. Rabih Bardawill, who mastered both CASC compositions, is credited in the English zine and absent from the Vietnamese one. ALAB and Ad Hoc Housing were on the book proposal in 2023 and are not on the current website. The sound map has no contributor identity at all — no account, no name, not even an optional field.

Nobody removed any of them. The lists drifted. A frame that is re-made rather than held loses people quietly, and the loss looks like nothing at all until somebody counts.

I value this principle because it is the one this practice fails most often and most invisibly, and because Terranova's account of free labour cannot be answered rhetorically — only with an actual licence, an actual credit model, and an actual deletion route.

Withdraw

The frame is proven by what continues without you.

Reinterpreting Narrowness began as five practices and one evening. It is now twenty-five practices, and they went on meeting without us. We did not convene the later gatherings and were not always told about them. The community that formed inside our frame outlived our holding of it, and detached.

Meike Schalk and Apolonija Sustersic built a garden in an Edinburgh close under an exhibition permit, describing their own role as "intermediaries in a process of self-organisation". When the exhibition closed, the residents removed the pots, planted permanent beds and secured a permit of their own. The frame-holder withdrew and the frame was taken over.

I value this principle because it is the only evidence a practice like this can honestly offer. Not that people enjoyed it. Not that eighty people came. That the thing continued in our absence and stopped requiring us.

05 — Two ways of not knowing

Two relations to not-knowing, and I have not decided whether they can coexist.

Between August 2025 and August 2026 I took leave from the candidature to direct art production for a contemporary art biennale and manage production for two others.

In the practice, not-knowing is the condition you stay inside long enough for something to arrive. In production, not-knowing is a risk with a cost and a date attached, and the entire craft consists of eliminating it early. Both are forms of professional seriousness. They are not compatible on the same afternoon.

These are two non-overlapping communities of practice with different standards for good work and different economies of recognition. I belong to both. What earns respect in one is close to invisible in the other.

The leave cost the schedule twelve months and gave the research its comparative structure. I am recording the question as open rather than resolving it for the panel's comfort.

06 — What continues, and what that proves

Continuation after withdrawal is the evidence — and on its own it proves nothing.

The discriminating question is not did it continue but what powers transferred. Every project plotted on the two variables the practice actually turns — requested by the panel at Confirmation in April 2024 and again in August 2024, and drawn here for the first time.

The failures sit where the two axes disagree — where a long hold meets a high release. That quadrant is editing, not hosting, and it is the operation this practice has not yet learned.

Kwon ran this experiment and refused the conclusion. Five of the six architects first invited to Reinterpreting Narrowness had been at university together — so the competing explanation is inside my own material, and imprinting predicts my exact result. What survives the objection is a different test: Park Fiction won "direct control over the finances"; Peterlee left "a heritage centre rather than a living archive." Ostrom's condition is a standing forum owned by the people in it, meeting on a known schedule, with the authority to change the rules. And from the most hostile witness, Bishop: "At a certain point, art has to hand over to other institutions."

The Rare Sea Gallery monthly workshops, from October 2026, are the first case where that transfer has been designed in advance rather than found afterwards.

What this does not prove. As of this submission, nothing has yet been amended without me. And the negative case belongs here too: the Gặp Series is three years old with no public record of a single session — no date, no venue, no guest list published anywhere. This section is written to be capable of reporting a failure, and at Milestone 2 it largely does.

07 — Holding the frame

The method, arrived at rather than assumed — and the exhibition built as a frame.

Ho Chi Minh City, late 2028. Not a retrospective.

  • The eighteen categories occupy a wall at full size, with Announcements and Signals empty and labelled as empty, and the suppressed Heritage text readable beside it.
  • The archive is audible in the room, unmixed and unsequenced, at whatever length each recording was made — the opposite of the 2022 dark room, and shown against it.
  • The Gặp Series nomination chain is drawn on the wall and left open, with the next nomination made during the exhibition.
  • Contributors named. Everyone. The licence question answered in public rather than in a footnote.
  • The Reinterpreting Narrowness community is invited to use the space for one of its own gatherings, which we do not programme.

Scheduled ahead of the final writing deadline on purpose. In this model the exhibition is not documentation of a completed argument; it is the last experiment, and the final chapter cannot close until it has run.
