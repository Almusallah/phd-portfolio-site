01 — Never quite dark

I believe that reality is an artifact our perception creates.

I mean that plainly. The city I walk around in is not a thing sitting there waiting to be recorded accurately. It is something produced, continuously, in the encounter between a place and an instrument — a body, a habit, a camera, a category, a language. Change the instrument and you get a different city, and neither version is the false one.

That is not a dive into subjectivity. It is the opposite. An impression cannot be examined. An instrument can: it can be built, published, handed to somebody else and argued with. So if the instrument is what does the making, the instrument is the thing I want to try to examine — and I believe this might be a method rather than a confession. It is also why I tried to organise the sections below around what the practice does rather than what it has made.

And the city gets its share. If perception makes the city, does the place contribute anything — or is this idealism with a camera? The load-bearing word is encounter, and an encounter has two sides. What the instrument does is not invent. It selects, from a place already denser than any instrument can take, and that surplus is not an inconvenience: it is the evidence the place is there, doing its own part. I know it is there by the resistance it puts up — recordings arrive that no category fits, categories fill with things I did not predict, a street refuses to sound the way the scheme says it should, and the scheme is what has to move.

By instrument I mean anything standing between a place and a perception of it, shaping what comes through: a body, a habit, a lens, a category list, a language. On this account the difference between a good and a bad instrument is not accuracy but whether it is legible enough to be worked against. A design criterion rather than a philosophical one, which is why I think it can be examined rather than only argued about.

Three conditions run under all of it, and none of them was chosen. The night, when the day’s noise in the mind goes quiet and attention can stay on the details — the hours when this city expresses herself best. Not having the language, so every name passes through somebody else and naming never feels natural. Not belonging, which keeps every habit visible as a habit — nothing here has ever been obvious to me.

The tools came from working inside those conditions, and I think they are one family. The frame, in every medium: the photograph’s edges, the thirty-six-second glimpse, the blurred image that carries what I feel better than a sharp one — the tool for researching that layer. The dark room in 2022 was a frame too, staged: eyes covered, eight minutes, an experiment on perception. And naming, the frame’s twin: the eleven series I invented and then went looking for, the categories of the sound map, the titles that arrive in two languages. The instrument is not any one of these tools. It is the whole way of working — and the map is where that way of working became a thing somebody else can hold.

Never quite dark: the night here is not less city but more. The noise the day leaves in your mind goes quiet, and you can stay on the details. Saigon at night is neon, people in the street, conviviality — the city expressing herself best. The night does not subtract; it amplifies.

Stalker and Francesco Careri are the closest precedent for the method: Careri writes that the walk builds nothing and yet transforms a place and its meanings, and teaches a course conducted entirely by walking, getting lost and wasting time. What does not transfer is the ground. Stalker walked terrain vague — peripheries, largely abandoned. What I walk is the opposite: dense, occupied, in constant use, a street life that is nobody's leftover. A walk down a working sidewalk answers to everyone on it. Re-aiming the method at occupied ground is what I am trying to do.

The literature theorises this territory and never builds anything for it. The Situationists make ambiance researchable from 1955 and rule out the last hours of the night. Careri and Stalker carry walking into the terrain vague, by day. Nick Dunn makes the night his object and names the shift away from the eye — then declines the instrument on principle: the one transferable instruction in Dark Matters is "go and be", and what attention makes in his nights stays private, gone by morning. I recognise the instruction, because it is half of my own method. Go and be; then build what holds it, so somebody else can enter. None of them builds the second half. That is where I am trying to go.

What I think I am reaching for — and I hold this loosely — is an instrument that helps me describe what I feel about this city, not only what is there. Blurred pictures have entered my way of capturing it: a blurred image expresses something a sharp one does not, and it has become a way of recording what I feel rather than only what I see. I recognise a similar attempt in Andrew Stiff's video work on the hẻms. Is that feeling enhanced by the blur itself? Is it enhanced by the distortion in the recordings? And underneath everything, the question this whole journey keeps opening: what if I built an instrument that recorded not what is there, but everyone's perception of it?

Once I photographed a banner for the new metro that said an toàn — tiện lợi — văn minh: safe, convenient, civilised. I keep wondering which way that runs: do the narratives build people's desire, or does people's desire build the narratives? Elite living, says the banner on the real-estate hoarding. The city is already covered in instruments that write its story for it — so maybe the tool worth building is the opposite one: something that invites people to create their own narrative.

The organising principle of sections 02 and 04 — two movements, each named by technique rather than by project — is a debt to Géraldine Borio's Parallel Works (RMIT, 2020), and to the principle only.

02 — A way to discover

Understanding through doing — how a practice comes to know a place it does not belong to.

Five techniques. The projects appear across all of them, doing different work in each.

Walking

Arriving on foot, at length, and usually late.

The same procedure in every city I have lived in: arrive somewhere I do not know, walk it at length, photograph what stops me, find the people who seem to be doing something interesting. Only then make anything. I did not recognise it as a procedure until recently.

What it costs. It is the only technique that produces material I did not go looking for.

Recording

Taking the glimpse rather than the survey.

The median recording on the sound map is thirty-six seconds. Seventy-eight per cent are under a minute. These are not field recordings in the ethnographic sense; nobody is documenting an hour of a market to capture its structure. The archive is made of attention spans.

Thirty-six seconds establishes a scene and cannot establish a process. You cannot hear a transaction complete or a street empty.

What it costs. It records what stopped me, which is a fact about attention rather than about the city.

  • CASC — 78 Saigon, 74 Hanoi
  • Sound map — 157 recordings, 2h12m

Collaborating

Nothing here was made alone.

Crafting A Sonic City was made with HUM Studios and mastered by Rabih Bardawill; the zine was designed by Jinny DH, and its Vietnamese edition translated by Khả-Nhân Trần and Khôi Minh; the dark rooms were built with the Blind Associations of Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Dạo Nghe happened inside MORUA's Trà đá Party. TUAR was run with the educators of Little Em's. The map goes to Nổ Cái Bùm with Vu's performance, and to Rare Sea Gallery from October.

What it costs. Every project carries other people's names, and showing the work means naming them.

Producing

Making a lot, together, and in public.

Between 2022 and 2025, Afra and I kept up a rhythm I can only see now: two sound exhibitions in two cities inside three weeks; an architecture week; a workshop with five-year-olds that ended in an exhibition; a sound walk; a zine, then its Vietnamese edition; three more zines in a single year; talks in Saigon, Bangkok and Trento; and the map. The rhythm is the method — short cycles, published, then the next one.

What it costs. The book we attempted never saw the light because we were chasing parameters of perfection and overcuration that do not exist. And everything gets recorded somehow — audio, photographs — but the records stay unpublished. The publishing runs behind the making.

Earning trust

Slowly, in person, before anything is asked.

None of this was available cold. The architects opened their studios to the public because we had been visiting them for months first. Little Em's trusted us with fifteen children at a roundabout. The Blind Associations worked with us in both cities. The practitioners in the Gặp Series let a two-person practice with no institution behind it sit in their workshops and ask questions. I do not have a method for this; it runs on time and presence, and it is how a practice comes to know a place it does not belong to.

What it costs. Trust does not scale and cannot be transferred. Whatever else in this practice somebody could pick up and use, this part they would have to build themselves.

03 — The instrument

The instrument is the authored work. Not the recordings it holds, and not any one state of its classification.

Saigon_Miền Tây Sound Map, 2025– · 157 recordings · 18 categories · open contribution.

The instrument, live. Not documentation — the map itself. Open it in its own window ↗

It began four years before it existed, in a dark room. Crafting A Sonic City, 2022: an audience with their eyes covered, played the city. That project worked and then it was over. An evening in a dark room is a very efficient way of making an argument and a very inefficient way of keeping one. The recordings went back onto a hard drive. Nothing had been made available to anybody who was not in the room.

Everything technical about the map answers that one problem: how do you make an atmosphere available to someone who was not there?

The recordings are evidence. The instrument is the work — the map, the categories as they currently stand, and the path a contribution takes. Publishing it is a claim, made in public, about what this city is made of, and everything that arrives afterwards either fits it or strains against it. The strain is data.

The scheme, and where it came from

In the order they are authored in — not alphabetical, and the order is itself a decision: Food & Drink · Sidewalks · Street Vendors · Music · Home · Work · Repairing · Conversations · Vehicles · Markets · Shops · Play & Leisure · Ritual & Ceremony · Announcements & Signals · Infrastructure & Utilities · Nature · Waterways · Background.

Every change to the scheme leaves a dated trace, which is why its intellectual history is recoverable at all. Largest by count: Sidewalks 25, Music 21, Conversations 17, Background 14, Street Vendors 13. Forty-two of 157 recordings carry Vietnamese titles — 27% overall, but nine of thirteen in Street Vendors.

And the scheme has a dated turning point. In May 2026 the catch-all category went, and Announcements & Signals arrived in its place. Without a catch-all there is nowhere for the unclassifiable to quietly go — the scheme has to be right, or be changed — and the new category has held zero recordings ever since. An author-invented slot with no occupant, offered publicly, waiting for material that has not come. The emptiest thing in the archive and the loudest evidence that the categories were authored rather than derived.

How they were derived. Not inductively from a corpus and not deductively from a theory: authored in advance, out of five years of walking and photographing this city, then corrected by what arrived. A weaker-sounding origin than either alternative, and the honest one — the first list was a proposition about what Ho Chi Minh City is made of, written by someone who had been paying attention and had no authority to write it.

Eighteen is not derived from anything. It is where the scheme stands after a sequence of dated decisions. A scheme whose number was derived — from a theory, a survey, an existing taxonomy — would be claiming a warrant it does not have. What I can defend is not the number but the trail: every change to the scheme carries a date and a reason, and that is what makes it an examinable artefact rather than a personal preference presented as a structure.

Three decisions that carry the argument

Sidewalks is the largest category. Not traffic, which everyone predicts; not markets, which the tourist literature predicts. A scheme that let the pavement be a subheading of street would have been a scheme that could not see its own subject.

The median recording is thirty-six seconds. A position, not a limit. The unit of this archive is a glimpse rather than a survey.

There is no Noise category, and the absence is published. To open one is to accept the whole machinery that treats urban sound as nuisance — the decibel map, the complaint, the ordinance. That machinery exists and it is not stupid. It is not what this instrument is for. The refusal is a refusal to let the intangible layer be entered in a ledger where its only available value is negative.

How it actually works

And the base is wrong. The map sits on an ordinary street map, while everything I argue says the street grid is not this city's ground — what I record is alleys, thresholds and times of day. Whether redrawing that base is a distraction or this project's most honest drawing is an open question I carry into the milestone.

And a limit I want to state myself, before somebody else finds it. The categories are titled in Vietnamese, but they were not thought in Vietnamese — they were authored in English and translated, and have not been back-translated or tested with native speakers as a scheme. Until that is done, the Vietnamese titling is a courtesy at the surface of an instrument authored elsewhere, and I should not describe it as more than that.

Each recording is a pin at the coordinates where it was made; category determines the pin. The visual density of a district is a direct read-out of where I have been — an honest map of my own range rather than of the city's. The player gives title, category, date, duration and the audio, and deliberately no waveform: a waveform invites the measuring attention this archive exists to hold off, and makes a thirty-six-second glimpse look like a specimen.

Submissions enter a queue rather than the map, because the category decision on an incoming recording is the exact point where the authored scheme meets somebody else's ear. Three ways a submission fails to fit: misuse, which is my badly written label; genuine ambiguity, a sidewalk that is also a workshop, which here is most sidewalks; and the one that matters — a category I do not have. Two of the current eighteen arrived that way.

The titles record who was speaking when each sound arrived. There is a recording titled gâu gâu — a dog, barking, in an alley in District 4 — that I did not make and did not name: it came through the open upload, recorded and titled by somebody from the community. In English it would have been "woof". Some titles are mine, in Vietnamese sometimes and in English sometimes, with no rule behind the choice.

A published failure. The archive originally served WebM — smaller, technically better, and inaudible on Safari on iOS. For a period a public archive of a Southeast Asian city could not be heard on the most common way of getting online in that city. Migrated to MP3, June 2026. The format decision was a distribution decision wearing technical clothes, and I did not see it until somebody could not hear it.

Its live present

The map is not waiting for 2028. It enters Nổ Cái Bùm with a zine and a sound performance by Vu, a sound practitioner building his own modular synthesiser — and the person who told me I had stopped making recordings and started making an instrument. From October, a collaboration with Rare Sea Gallery: monthly sound walks and workshops. A monthly room, on a schedule, with recorders in it, is the only place where the categories can be argued with by somebody other than me.

The instrument is the work, and it is not mine alone

Most of my hours do not go into recording. They go into building and improving the thing itself: the software, the categories, what a recording is allowed to carry, who can contribute, how it gets activated. Recordings can now hold a photograph alongside the audio, and deciding that took longer than making a hundred recordings. Vu — who stopped making music with instruments and started building the instrument — is the person who pointed out that I had done the same without noticing. The recordings are what the work produces. The instrument is the work. That sentence came from a conversation, not from me.

I build it daily, in conversation with an AI, the way Vu solders. I state that plainly because an argument that the instrument must be published cannot then decline to publish how the instrument was made.

And the instrument does not work when it sits. The map does its real work when Afra builds activation events around it — when it is staged, played, walked with. It is not an archive that rests; it is an instrument that has to be performed, and performing it is not my skill. Officine Gặp names a two-person operation in which the building and the activating are different jobs held by different people.

Afra is also a doctoral candidate now, in the same school — her research examines {co}curating as an emerging urban practice, confirmed at PRS Asia in March 2026 — and the fair question is where hers ends and mine begins. From my side the line looks like this: I build the part underneath — the platform, what a recording can carry, who can press upload — and those decisions already decide some of what can happen, whether I intend it or not. Afra works in front of it: the people, the occasions, what the map becomes in somebody else's hands. With Vu and the Mekong work I can leave the thing alone and wait for what comes back; Afra would rather begin by talking it through together, and I have stopped thinking one of those instincts is the correct one. Neither position works without the other, and the authorship question in this section is easier to ask honestly because there are two of us to disagree about it.

It grew under pressure

One fact I did not expect and cannot explain away. Almost the entire collection was made during the two years I was working on biennales — thirty-seven recordings in April, sixteen in May, eighty-eight in June — and that June was one of the heaviest production months of my working life. The slowest, least instrumental thing I do was at its most active in the months I was most scheduled. I think the recording, and the building, may have been how I metabolised the other work — which means the two ways of working I spent two years treating as alternatives were running simultaneously the whole time, and I did not notice.

The archive already here, and the field around it

An Urban Archive of District 4, Ho Chi Minh City has been running since September 2016 — public, browsable, holding video, photography, audio, drawing and mixed reality from the hẻms of Ward 14, over four collections and an open tag vocabulary. A public, categorised, contributable audiovisual archive of a district of this city, made nine years before mine. Any account of what the sound map contributes has to begin there.

It states its own position clearly, and it is not mine: "our archive is not so much a documentary of the hẻms, but one that responds to what we look at in the studio." Both of us are making an argument about the instrument, at opposite ends of the pipeline. Their creative act happens after capture, and their classification is a retrieval layer. Mine happens before capture — the categories were published in advance of most of the recordings now in them, they are closed rather than additive, dated, and their refusals are published alongside their contents. Nothing here is processed at all.

Nor does it stand alone. Andrew Stiff's Moving Experiences: Video Observations of Ho Chi Minh City (2021) films the hẻms and runs the footage through customised lenses and filters; his Peripheral Poetics trips (2026) do it from a motorbike. Thierry Bernard's More-Than-Noise: Listening with Saigon Soundscapes (2026) is a listening practice in this city finishing now. Stiff, Jahja, Bernard & Youn's Interdisciplinary Weavings (2026) takes the Kênh Tẻ canal into twenty-eight metres of film, environmental sound and cyanotype. Rachel Jahja's motorbike paper (2025) reports nine traverses of all nineteen districts, camera fixed behind the driver, screengrabs every five to ten seconds.

Put beside each other, these are all arguments about the instrument, and every one of them instruments capture — the lens, the filter, the mount, the interval, the route. None of them classifies. So the local field has thoroughly established that the instrument of capture is authorable, and has left the instrument of sorting almost untouched. That is a narrower claim to novelty, and I think a more defensible one, and it only becomes visible when the work next door is named.

The objection, given at full strength

My defence has been a split: the categories are mine, the collecting is theirs. That is Ida Halpern's position, restated almost word for word. Halpern recorded Kwakwaka'wakw songs across decades and was scrupulous about naming her singers. Once the recordings entered archival collections — as Dylan Robinson records in Hungry Listening — "Halpern is the only individual whose artistry is recognized." Intent did not enter into it.

Much of Robinson does not transfer: his argument runs on a seventy-year potlatch ban and on ownership by named hereditary rights holders, and there is no hereditary rights holder for a street. But strip the settler-colonial frame out entirely and three claims stand: the taxonomy is authored power; the collector accrues the attribution whatever the collector intends; and access-is-good is a premise, not a fact.

The answer is not a paragraph of acknowledgement — Robinson calls that a non-performative utterance. Three structural changes, dated: guest listening as the declared position, his term, not mine; a present-but-withheld state in the archive, so the instrument contains a page that says stop here; and a prescriptive score naming which categories carry protocol weight, who holds editorial authority over them, and how material can be reclassified or removed by whoever contributed it.

What this does not prove. Not that the categories are correct — correctness is not available; a scheme is a proposition about a city, judged by what it lets you see and what it costs you. Not that the archive is representative; the pin density says otherwise. And not that the transfer of authority above has occurred. As of this submission it is designed, partly built, and scheduled.

04 — A way to make with

Meeting through doing with — becoming part of a place rather than reporting on one.

Four principles. Values rather than a method — but each one is falsifiable, and two of them the practice has already failed.

Make the room,
not the content

Fix the format; leave the material to other people.

In 2022 the architects working on the tube house did not know each other. Vietnamese contemporary architecture was a set of serious practices operating in isolation, each with a following and almost no relation to the others. There was no room in which they were all present.

So we made the room. That is the whole of the intervention: a date, a venue, an invitation, a subject narrow enough that everyone present was already an expert in it, and a format that required people to travel between each other's studios rather than sit in rows. We printed something closer to a treasure map than a programme.

I keep coming back to this principle because I had no expertise to offer. I was a foreigner with no architectural training, in a city I had lived in for months. What I had was the ability to make a room in which people who did know would talk to each other in public.

Publish the rule

A frame is only accountable if its terms can be read.

Jo Freeman's argument holds here and it is the best defence I have: there is no structureless group, only groups whose structure is unacknowledged and therefore unaccountable. An explicit, published, amendable frame is more democratic than a facilitator's tacit charisma.

But it obliges me to write the frame down, and the Gặp Series is where I have not. Since 2023 we have been going to see practitioners whose way of working leaves room for slowness and reflection: architects, a designer making furniture from objects found on the street, an anthropologist of Vietnamese urbanism. The format is deliberately loose — five to fifteen people, no rigid structure, participants becoming the interviewers — because the point is the curiosity, not the protocol.

And the records stay unpublished. Every session was recorded somehow — audio, photographs — but no date, no venue, no guest list is published anywhere. What is missing is not the record and not a rule. It is the public, checkable account of the community this was assembling, which is the thing the panel has been asking me to name since Confirmation.

I keep coming back to this principle because it converts a good intention into something that can be checked by someone other than me — and because the failure here is not an admin oversight. A community of practice that exists only in my memory is not evidence.

Keep the hold short

Short cycles are held well. Long ones drift.

Holding a frame for an evening is hosting. Holding one for four years is editing, and editing means deciding on other people's behalf, repeatedly, in ways they can contest. These are different operations and I have only learned the first.

The evidence is in the formats. Three zines in a year is a rhythm. One book in four years is a stall. The Reinterpreting Narrowness book is something we attempted, and it never saw the light: we were chasing parameters of perfection and overcuration that do not exist.

I keep coming back to this principle because it explains a pattern I had been reading as a personal failure to finish things. It is more specific than that: this practice holds frames whose terms are published up front and whose holding period is short.

Withdraw

The frame is proven by what continues without you.

Reinterpreting Narrowness began as five practices and one evening. It is now twenty-five practices, and they went on meeting without us. We did not convene the later gatherings and were not always told about them. The community that formed inside our frame outlived our holding of it, and detached.

Meike Schalk and Apolonija Sustersic built a garden in an Edinburgh close under an exhibition permit, describing their own role as "intermediaries in a process of self-organisation". When the exhibition closed, the residents removed the pots, planted permanent beds and secured a permit of their own. The frame-holder withdrew and the frame was taken over.

I keep coming back to this principle because it is the only evidence a practice like this can honestly offer. Not that people enjoyed it. Not that eighty people came. That the thing continued in our absence and stopped requiring us.

TUAR — Tactical Urbanism at Turtle Lake, 2023. Fifteen children aged four and five, from Little Em's preschool, observing and reimagining Hồ Con Rùa. They read the site from the inside out: the middle was the place, and the traffic was weather. It is the inverse of how every plan of that location is drawn, and it is the only project where my frame was the only frame in the room.

05 — Two ways of not knowing

Two relations to not-knowing, and I have not decided whether they can coexist.

Between August 2025 and August 2026 I took leave from the candidature to direct art production for a contemporary art biennale and manage production for two others.

In the practice, not-knowing is the condition you stay inside long enough for something to arrive. In production, not-knowing is a risk with a cost and a date attached, and the entire craft consists of eliminating it early. Both are forms of professional seriousness. They are not compatible on the same afternoon.

These are two non-overlapping communities of practice with different standards for good work and different economies of recognition. I belong to both. What earns respect in one is close to invisible in the other.

The leave cost the schedule twelve months and gave the research its comparative structure. I am leaving the question open.

06 — What continues, and what that proves

Continuation after withdrawal is the evidence — and on its own it proves nothing.

The question I have ended up asking is not did it continue but what powers transferred.

Kwon ran this experiment and refused the conclusion. Five of the six architects first invited to Reinterpreting Narrowness had been at university together — so the competing explanation is inside my own material, and imprinting predicts my exact result. What survives the objection is a different test: Park Fiction won "direct control over the finances"; Peterlee left "a heritage centre rather than a living archive." Ostrom's condition is a standing forum owned by the people in it, meeting on a known schedule, with the authority to change the rules. And from the most hostile witness, Bishop: "At a certain point, art has to hand over to other institutions."

The Rare Sea Gallery monthly workshops, from October 2026, are the first case where that transfer has been designed in advance rather than found afterwards.

What this does not prove. As of this submission, nothing has yet been amended without me. And the negative case belongs here too: the Gặp Series is three years old and every session was recorded somehow — but nothing is published: no date, no venue, no guest list anywhere public. At Milestone 2, most of what there is to report is failure.

07 — Holding the frame

The method, arrived at rather than assumed — and the exhibition built as a frame.

Ho Chi Minh City, late 2028. Not a retrospective.

  • The categories occupy a wall at full size, as the scheme stands that year — with Announcements and Signals empty and labelled as empty.
  • The archive is audible in the room, unmixed and unsequenced, at whatever length each recording was made — the opposite of the 2022 dark room, and shown against it.
  • The Gặp Series nomination chain is drawn on the wall and left open, with the next nomination made during the exhibition.
  • Contributors named. Everyone. The licence question answered in public rather than in a footnote.
  • The Reinterpreting Narrowness community is invited to use the space for one of its own gatherings, which we do not programme.

Scheduled ahead of the final writing deadline on purpose. In this model the exhibition is not documentation of a completed argument; I want it to be the last experiment, and I do not think the final chapter can close until it has run.

The two tracks. The Hanoi and Saigon compositions from the 2022 dark rooms, with HUM Studios. Every decision in them was ours — which is what the 2028 room gives up.

What none of this proves. That any of this changed the city. I am not going to claim it did. What I can show is narrower: that frames we built went on being used after we stopped holding them, and that where we stopped holding them badly, they drifted. Both halves are here.
